# Field Guide: Preparing a Home for a 72-Hour Power Outage

Ready an ordinary household to manage safely and comfortably through an outage up to 72 hours. Assumes a temperate climate, no backup generator. Do the sections in order; earlier ones matter most.

## Water
- Store 4 litres per person per day = 12 litres per person for 72 hours: half for drinking, half for cooking and basic hygiene.
- Food-grade containers, out of direct sunlight; replace every 6 months.
- Storm forecast with warning: also fill the bathtub, for flushing toilets and washing, never drinking.

## Food
- Dedicated shelf of no-cook non-perishable food for 3 days: canned beans, canned fish, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit, shelf-stable milk.
- Own a manual can opener; an electric one is useless.
- Eat in order: perishable fridge food, then freezer, then the emergency shelf.

## Refrigerator and freezer
- Safe while unopened: fridge about 4 hours; full freezer about 48 hours; half-full freezer about 24 hours.
- So keep the freezer consolidated and full, filling spare space with containers of frozen water. Keep both doors closed as much as possible; tape a reminder note to each.
- Discard perishable food over 2 hours above 4 degrees Celsius; when in doubt, throw it out.

## Light
- Primary light: battery-powered LED lanterns or headlamps. Avoid candles entirely, a leading cause of house fires during outages.
- At least one light per person plus two spare battery sets each; one headlamp by each bed, one lantern in the kitchen, placed to be found in the dark.

## Heat and safety
- Never run a camping stove, barbecue, or generator indoors, in a garage, or near a window: each produces carbon monoxide, which is odourless and can be fatal.
- Install at least one battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm on each floor where people sleep.
- Cold weather: gather everyone in one small room on a south-facing side, door closed; layered clothing and blankets are safer than any improvised heater.
- If the indoor temperature falls below 10 degrees Celsius and you cannot maintain warmth, relocate to a warming centre or a neighbour's home.

## Communication and information
- Battery-powered or hand-crank radio for emergency broadcasts; know your local emergency station's frequency before the outage.
- When a storm is forecast, charge every phone and power bank: a full 20,000 milliamp-hour bank recharges a typical phone roughly 4 times. At the outage's start, set phones to low-power mode.
- Pre-agree one out-of-area contact whom every household member texts, because texts get through congested networks more reliably than voice calls.

## Medical needs
- First-aid kit plus a 7-day supply of every prescription medication; rotate them so they stay in date.
- Refrigerated medication such as insulin generally stays usable at room temperature for up to 28 days, but confirm your specific medication with a pharmacist beforehand.
- Write your pharmacy, doctor, and poison-control numbers on paper, because a dead phone is not a phone book.

## When the power returns
- Wait 5 minutes before switching major appliances back on, so the grid can stabilise.
- Check the freezer: food that still contains ice crystals can be safely refrozen.
- Restock everything used within one week, starting with water and batteries.
- Write a one-page note about what was missing or awkward, and fix at least one of those gaps before the next outage.
