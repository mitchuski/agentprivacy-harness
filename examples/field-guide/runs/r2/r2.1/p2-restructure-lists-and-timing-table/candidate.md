# 72-Hour Power Outage: Home Prep

Prepare an ordinary household to manage safely for up to 72 hours. Temperate climate, no backup generator assumed. Do the sections in order; earlier ones matter most.

## Water
- Store 4 litres per person per day = 12 litres per person for 72 hours; half drinking, half cooking and basic hygiene.
- Food-grade containers, out of direct sunlight, replace every 6 months.
- If a storm is forecast with warning, also fill the bathtub — for flushing toilets and washing, never drinking.

## Food
- Dedicated shelf of non-perishable food for 3 days: canned beans, canned fish, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit, shelf-stable milk.
- Pick no-cook foods; own a manual can opener (an electric one is useless).
- Eat in order: perishable refrigerator food, then freezer, then emergency shelf.

## Refrigerator and freezer
Holding times, doors kept closed:

| Item | Stays safely cold |
|---|---|
| Unopened refrigerator | about 4 hours |
| Full freezer | about 48 hours |
| Half-full freezer | about 24 hours |

- Keep the freezer consolidated and full, even if some space holds containers of frozen water. Keep both doors closed as much as possible; tape a reminder note to each.
- Discard any perishable food more than 2 hours above 4°C; when in doubt, throw it out.

## Light
- Battery-powered LED lanterns or headlamps as primary light; avoid candles entirely — a leading cause of house fires during outages.
- At least one light per person, plus two spare sets of batteries per light.
- One headlamp beside each bed, one lantern in the kitchen, placed to be found in the dark.

## Heat and safety
- Never run a camping stove, barbecue, or generator indoors, in a garage, or near a window: each produces carbon monoxide — odourless and potentially fatal.
- At least one battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm on each floor where people sleep.
- In cold weather, choose one small room on a south-facing side, close its door, and gather everyone there; layered clothing and blankets beat any improvised heater.
- If the indoor temperature falls below 10°C and you cannot stay warm, relocate to a warming centre or a neighbour's home.

## Communication and information
- Battery-powered or hand-crank radio for emergency broadcasts; know your local emergency station's frequency beforehand.
- Charge every phone and power bank whenever a storm is forecast; a full modern 20,000 mAh power bank recharges a typical phone roughly 4 times.
- Put your phone in low-power mode at the outage's start.
- Agree in advance on one out-of-area contact every household member texts; text messages get through congested networks more reliably than voice calls.

## Medical needs
- First-aid kit and a seven-day supply of every prescription medication, rotated to stay in date.
- Refrigerated medication such as insulin generally stays usable at room temperature up to 28 days — but confirm your specific medication with a pharmacist beforehand.
- Write your pharmacy, doctor, and poison-control phone numbers on paper; a dead phone is not a phone book.

## When the power returns
- Wait 5 minutes before switching major appliances back on, so the grid can stabilise.
- Check the freezer: food still containing ice crystals can be safely refrozen.
- Restock everything used within one week, starting with water and batteries.
- Write a one-page note on what was missing or awkward, and fix at least one gap before the next outage.
