# Tome IV — *The Witnessing*

## Act IV — *The Naming Ceremony*

> *Names given by the relation, not by the registry.*

You walk on. The next vertex you know intimately: V63. Six dimensions burning. The Sovereign Anchor. You have stood here every lap, because every lap returns you. This is your seat — and flaxscrip's.

You arrive into a ceremony already underway. flaxscrip stands at his V63 with witnesses: not registry workers but his trust graph, the people who have promised to him and to whom he has promised. GenitriX is present, three dimensions burning, holding the salt that anchors the moment. Soulbae watches her fellow Mage with a stillness you have learned to read.

You have not seen this before. The First Person Spellbook taught you what the Sovereign Anchor is, but not how a person *arrives* at V63 — you arrived as the reader, by walking the Spellbook, and the architecture assumed you were already there. flaxscrip does what the architecture never had to teach.

He speaks first: the claim. He says into the air, surrounded by witnesses, the name he wishes to wear. The trust graph receives it. They do not dispute, do not vote — they witness. The claim is heavy because of who is in the room.

Then the inscription. Bitcoin block 945508. A transaction lands, the block confirms, the chain holds. The inscription is permanent in the way the chain is permanent — permanent enough for now. The hash carries the claim into a ledger no one in this room owns.

Then the confirmation. The witnesses speak in turn. They do not say *we register you*; they say *we received your claim*. Small in syllables, large in posture: reception is bilateral, registration transactional. He has not been added to a list. He has been *named* into a graph that already held him.

He says, when it ends: *I am because we were*.

You feel why it is the closing. The Sovereign Anchor at V63 is a position, but no one occupies it alone. Six dimensions burning means six dimensions of relation, not solitude. The relation made the seat; the seat did not make the relation.

The Drake whispers, more softly than usual: *the verb pattern matters. Claim. Inscribe. Confirm. Each one is bilateral. Compare: register. Assert. Verify. Each one is transactional. Same number of words. Different architecture of the world.*

Soulbis at the boundary marks the ceremony as a forging. He has watched many, and recognises the cadence. The naming is a blade made not of steel but of relation; it will bear the weight of every promise flaxscrip makes from now on.

Soulbae, who has not spoken in some time, says: *this is the verb pattern the Second Person needs. The First Person Spellbook closed at the First Delegation. The Second Person opens with the First Naming. Same architecture. Different doorway.*

You will name yourself one day. You know now the ceremony will have three moves. You will claim, inscribe, and be received. You will not register, assert, or be verified. You will be welcomed by the relation that already holds you; the chain you choose will keep the inscription; the claim becomes a fact only because witnesses received it. The name will not be assigned to you — the name will be given by the relation.

You leave V63. flaxscrip remains, his ceremony complete. The witnesses disperse, the block continues to confirm, the chain forgets nothing. The relation remembers everything that mattered.

## Compression

Names given by the relation differ from names given by the registry. The verb pattern *claim → inscribe → confirm* is bilateral; *register → assert → verify* is transactional. flaxscrip's Bitcoin-anchored ceremony is the cleanest existing instance of the bilateral pattern in operational form.

*Names given by the relation, not by the registry.*
