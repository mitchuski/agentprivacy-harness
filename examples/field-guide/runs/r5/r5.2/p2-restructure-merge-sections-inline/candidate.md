# Field Guide: Prepare a Home for a 72-Hour Power Outage

Ordinary household, temperate climate, no generator. Work the sections in order; earlier ones matter most.

**Water.** 4 L/person/day = 12 L/person over 72 h; half drinking, half cooking and hygiene. Store in food-grade containers out of direct sunlight; replace every 6 months. Storm forecast with warning: also fill the bathtub — for flushing and washing, never drinking.

**Food.** Dedicated shelf, 3 days, no cooking: canned beans, canned fish, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit, shelf-stable milk. Keep a manual can opener (electric is useless). Eat in order: fridge perishables first, then freezer, then the emergency shelf.

**Refrigerator and freezer.** Safe holding: unopened fridge ~4 h; full freezer ~48 h; half-full ~24 h — so keep it consolidated and full, even with containers of frozen water. Keep both doors closed; tape a reminder note to each. Discard any perishable over 2 h above 4°C; when in doubt, throw it out.

**Light.** Primary source: battery LED lanterns or headlamps; no candles — a leading cause of outage house fires. At least one light per person plus two spare battery sets each. One headlamp by each bed, one lantern in the kitchen; place them findable in the dark.

**Heat and safety.** Never run a camping stove, barbecue, or generator indoors, in a garage, or near a window: carbon monoxide is odourless and can be fatal. Install at least one battery CO alarm on each floor where people sleep. Cold: pick one small room on a south-facing side, close the door, gather everyone; layered clothing and blankets beat any improvised heater. If indoors falls below 10°C and you can't stay warm, relocate to a warming centre or neighbour's.

**Communication.** Battery or hand-crank radio for broadcasts; learn your local emergency station's frequency beforehand. Storm forecast: charge every phone and power bank — a full 20,000 mAh bank recharges a typical phone ~4 times. At the start set phones to low-power mode. Agree in advance on one out-of-area contact everyone texts; texts beat voice calls on congested networks.

**Medical.** First-aid kit plus a 7-day supply of every prescription; rotate to keep them in date. Refrigerated medication such as insulin generally lasts up to 28 days at room temperature — confirm your specific drug with a pharmacist beforehand. Write pharmacy, doctor, and poison-control numbers on paper; a dead phone is no phone book.

**When power returns.** Wait 5 minutes before switching major appliances on, letting the grid stabilise. Check the freezer: food still holding ice crystals can be refrozen safely. Restock everything used within one week, water and batteries first. Write a one-page note on what was missing or awkward, and fix at least one gap before the next outage.
