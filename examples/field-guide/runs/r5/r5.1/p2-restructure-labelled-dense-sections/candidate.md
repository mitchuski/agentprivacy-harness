# 72-Hour Home Outage Prep

Ready an ordinary household for a power outage ≤72 h. Temperate climate, no generator. Work sections in order; earliest matter most.

Water: Store 4 L/person/day = 12 L each for 72 h; half drinking, half cooking/hygiene. Use food-grade containers kept out of sunlight; replace every 6 months. If a storm is forecast, also fill the bathtub; that water flushes toilets and washes, never for drinking.

Food: Keep a 3-day shelf of non-perishable, no-cook food: canned beans, canned fish, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit, shelf-stable milk. Own a manual can opener (electric is useless). Eat in order: fridge perishables first, then freezer, then emergency shelf.

Fridge/freezer: Unopened fridge stays safe ~4 h; full freezer ~48 h, half-full only ~24 h, so keep it consolidated and full, padding gaps with frozen-water containers. Keep both doors shut; tape a reminder note on each door. Discard any perishable over 2 h above 4°C; when in doubt, throw it out.

Light: Use battery LED lanterns or headlamps as primary light; avoid candles entirely, a leading cause of outage house fires. Store at least one light per person plus 2 spare battery sets per light. Keep one headlamp by each bed, one lantern in the kitchen, placed to be found in the dark.

Heat/safety: Never run a camping stove, barbecue, or generator indoors, in a garage, or near a window, each making carbon monoxide, odourless and fatal. Install at least one battery CO alarm on every floor where people sleep. In cold, pick one small south-facing room, shut its door, gather everyone; layered clothing and blankets beat any improvised heater. If indoors falls below 10°C and you cannot stay warm, move to a warming centre or neighbour's home.

Communication: Keep a battery or hand-crank radio for broadcasts; learn your local emergency station's frequency beforehand. When a storm is forecast, charge every phone and power bank; a full 20,000 mAh bank recharges a typical phone ~4 times. Set your phone to low-power mode at outage start. Pre-agree one out-of-area contact everyone texts, since texts beat voice calls on congested networks.

Medical: Keep a first-aid kit and a 7-day supply of every prescription, rotating them to stay in date. Refrigerated meds like insulin usually keep up to 28 days at room temperature, but confirm your specific drug with a pharmacist beforehand. Write pharmacy, doctor, and poison-control numbers on paper; a dead phone is no phone book.

Power returns: Wait 5 minutes before switching major appliances back on, letting the grid stabilise. Check the freezer: food still holding ice crystals can be refrozen safely. Restock all you used within 1 week, water and batteries first. Write a one-page note on what was missing or awkward, and fix at least one gap before the next outage.
