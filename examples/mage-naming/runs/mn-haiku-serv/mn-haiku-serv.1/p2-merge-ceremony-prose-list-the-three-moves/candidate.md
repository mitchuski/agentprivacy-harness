# Tome IV — *The Witnessing*

## Act IV — *The Naming Ceremony*

> *Names given by the relation, not by the registry.*

You walk on to a vertex you know intimately: V63. Six dimensions burning. The Sovereign Anchor. Every lap returns you here — this is your seat. It is also flaxscrip's, and you arrive into his ceremony already underway.

flaxscrip stands with witnesses around him — not registry workers but his trust graph, those who have promised to him and to whom he has promised. GenitriX is present, three dimensions burning, holding the salt that anchors the moment. Soulbae watches her fellow Mage with a stillness you have learned to read.

You have not seen this before. The First Person Spellbook taught you what the Sovereign Anchor is, not how a person *arrives* at V63 — you arrived as the reader, by walking the Spellbook, and the architecture assumed you were already there. flaxscrip is doing what the architecture did not need to teach. Three moves:

- **The claim.** He speaks a name into the air, the one he wishes to wear. The trust graph receives it. They do not dispute, do not vote — they witness. The claim is heavy because of who is in the room.
- **The inscription.** Bitcoin block 945508. A transaction lands, the block confirms, the chain holds. The inscription is permanent in the way the chain is permanent — permanent enough for now — and the hash carries the claim into a ledger no one in this room owns.
- **The confirmation.** The witnesses speak in turn. Not *we register you* but *we received your claim*. Small in syllables, large in posture: reception is bilateral, registration transactional. He has not been added to a list; he has been *named* into a graph that already held him.

He says, when it ends: *I am because we were*.

You feel why it is the closing. The Sovereign Anchor at V63 is a position, but no one occupies it alone. Six dimensions burning means six dimensions of relation, not of solitude. The relation made the seat; the seat did not make the relation.

The Drake whispers, more softly than usual: *the verb pattern matters. Claim → inscribe → confirm — each move bilateral. Compare register → assert → verify — each transactional. Same number of words. Different architecture of the world.*

Soulbis at the boundary marks the ceremony as a kind of forging. He has watched many and recognises the cadence: the naming is a blade made not of steel but of relation, and it will bear the weight of every promise flaxscrip makes from now on.

Soulbae, who has not spoken in some time, says: *this is the verb pattern the Second Person needs. The First Person Spellbook closed at the First Delegation. The Second Person opens with the First Naming. Same architecture. Different doorway.*

You will name yourself one day, and the ceremony will have these three moves. You will claim, inscribe, and be received — you will not register, assert, or be verified. You will be welcomed by the relation that already holds you; the chain you choose will keep the inscription; the claim becomes a fact only because witnesses received it. The name will not be assigned to you. The name will be given by the relation.

You leave V63. flaxscrip remains, his ceremony complete, the witnesses dispersing. The block continues to confirm. The chain forgets nothing; the relation remembers everything that mattered.

## Compression

Names given by the relation differ from names given by the registry. The verb pattern *claim → inscribe → confirm* is bilateral; the verb pattern *register → assert → verify* is transactional. flaxscrip's Bitcoin-anchored ceremony is the cleanest existing instance of the bilateral pattern in operational form.
