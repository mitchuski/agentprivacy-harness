# Prepare a Home for a 72-Hour Power Outage

Ready your household to pass safely and comfortably through an outage up to seventy-two hours. Temperate climate, no backup generator. Work sections in order; earlier ones matter most.

## Water

Store four litres per person daily, twelve per person over seventy-two hours. Half for drinking, half for cooking and hygiene. Food-grade containers, out of direct sunlight, replaced every six months. If a storm looms, also fill the bathtub; that water flushes toilets and washes, never drink it.

## Food

Stock a three-day shelf of non-perishable food: canned beans, canned fish, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit, shelf-stable milk. Pick no-cook foods; keep a manual can opener; electric is useless. Eat perishable fridge food first, then freezer, then emergency shelf.

## Fridge/freezer

An unopened fridge keeps food safe about four hours. A full freezer holds about forty-eight hours, half-full only twenty-four, keep it consolidated and full, even with frozen-water containers. Keep both doors closed; tape a note to each. Discard perishable food over two hours above four degrees Celsius; when in doubt, throw it out.

## Light

Use battery LED lanterns or headlamps for primary light; avoid candles, a leading cause of house fires during outages. Store at least one light per person plus two spare battery sets each. Keep a headlamp by each bed, a lantern in the kitchen, findable in the dark.

## Heat/safety

Never run a camping stove, barbecue, or generator indoors, in a garage, or near a window: each makes carbon monoxide, odourless and fatal. Install at least one battery carbon-monoxide alarm on every floor where people sleep. In cold, pick one small south-facing room, shut its door, gather everyone; layered clothing and blankets beat any improvised heater. If indoor temperature drops below ten degrees Celsius and you cannot stay warm, move to a warming centre or neighbour's home.

## Communication

Keep a battery or hand-crank radio for emergency broadcasts; learn your local emergency station's frequency beforehand. Charge every phone and power bank when a storm looms; a full twenty-thousand-milliamp-hour bank recharges a typical phone about four times. Set your phone to low-power mode at the start; agree beforehand on one out-of-area contact everyone texts; texts beat voice calls on congested networks.

## Medical

Keep a first-aid kit and a seven-day supply of every prescription medication, rotating it to stay in date. Refrigerated medication like insulin stays usable at room temperature up to twenty-eight days, but confirm yours with a pharmacist first. Write your pharmacy, doctor, and poison-control numbers on paper; a dead phone is not a phone book.

## When power returns

Wait five minutes before switching major appliances on, letting the grid stabilise. Check the freezer: food holding ice crystals can be refrozen safely. Restock everything used within one week, starting with water and batteries. Write a one-page note on what was missing or awkward, and fix at least one gap before the next outage.
