# Home Prep for a 72-Hour Power Outage

Prepare an ordinary household to manage safely and comfortably through an outage of up to 72 hours. Assumes a temperate climate and no backup generator. Work the sections in order; earlier ones matter most.

## Water
Store 4 litres per person per day — 12 litres per person for 72 hours: half for drinking, half for cooking and basic hygiene. Use food-grade containers, keep them out of direct sunlight, and replace stored water every 6 months. If a storm is forecast, also fill the bathtub; bathtub water is for flushing toilets and washing, never drinking.

## Food and cold storage
Keep a dedicated shelf of non-perishable food for 3 days, needing no cooking: canned beans, canned fish, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit, shelf-stable milk. Own a manual can opener, since an electric one is useless. Eat perishables from the refrigerator first, then the freezer, and only then the emergency shelf. An unopened refrigerator stays safe about 4 hours; a full freezer holds a safe temperature about 48 hours, a half-full one only about 24, so keep the freezer consolidated and full, even with containers of frozen water. Keep both doors closed as much as possible and tape a reminder note to each. Discard any perishable more than 2 hours above 4°C; when in doubt, throw it out.

## Light
Use battery-powered LED lanterns or headlamps as your primary light; avoid candles entirely, a leading cause of house fires during outages. Store at least one light per person plus two spare sets of batteries for each. Keep one headlamp beside each bed and one lantern in the kitchen, placed where they can be found in the dark.

## Heat and safety
Never run a camping stove, barbecue, or generator indoors, in a garage, or near a window: each produces carbon monoxide, which is odourless and can be fatal. Install at least one battery-powered carbon-monoxide alarm on each floor where people sleep. In cold weather, pick one small room on the home's south-facing side, close its door, and gather everyone there; layered clothing and blankets beat any improvised heater. If indoor temperature falls below 10°C and you cannot stay warm, relocate to a warming centre or a neighbour's home.

## Communication
Keep a battery-powered or hand-crank radio for emergency broadcasts, and learn your local emergency station's frequency before the outage. Charge every phone and power bank whenever a storm is forecast; a full 20,000 mAh power bank recharges a typical phone about 4 times. Put your phone in low-power mode at the outage's start. Agree in advance on one out-of-area contact everyone will text, since texts beat voice calls on congested networks.

## Medical
Keep a first-aid kit and a 7-day supply of every prescription medication, rotating them to stay in date. Refrigerated medication such as insulin generally stays usable at room temperature up to 28 days, but confirm the rule for your medication with a pharmacist beforehand. Write your pharmacy, doctor, and poison-control numbers on paper, since a dead phone is not a phone book.

## When power returns
Wait 5 minutes before switching major appliances back on, letting the grid stabilise. Check the freezer: food still holding ice crystals can be safely refrozen. Restock everything used within one week, starting with water and batteries. Finally, write a one-page note on what was missing or awkward, and fix at least one gap before the next outage.
